# Capstone collaboration and data use

Reviewed on 25 November 2026 for the Imperial ML and AI Black-Box Optimisation
challenge. This records the wording I could verify and its implications for
the assessed work. The live Canvas pages redirected to sign-in during the
review. The Module 12 intake notes, archived course PDFs, final project
templates and a 23 November capture of the GitHub activity provide the
course-specific evidence below.

| Question | Evidence and interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| What data does the brief describe for a query? | The capstone FAQ explains loading the supplied starters and appending feedback from successive submissions. Page 6 refers to all available data in the same context as accumulated feedback. The portal guide describes your cohort, weekly input submission and the return for each function. These passages describe the official workflow. They leave third-party observations unaddressed. |
| May I pool current-cohort returns for assessed query selection? | The course asks learners to explain approaches, comment on peers’ reflections, and share a GitHub profile and project progress. I found no explicit permission for coordinated or retrospective pooling of scores to select assessed queries. Emeritus restricts sharing solutions and activities that improve or harm another student's scores. Coordinating evaluations for use by multiple entrants is particularly close to that restriction. |
| May I use an earlier cohort's returns? | The brief materials reviewed are silent on other cohorts. Historical scores could change the selection of an assessed query even when the older students are no longer competing. Matching starter arrays demonstrate comparability, and permission remains a separate question. |
| May I use public repositories? | The FAQ requires a public final GitHub repository. The profile activity encourages sharing progress and documenting your approach. The final templates ask for data provenance, citations and terms of use. Public visibility alone supplies neither programme permission for assessed reuse nor a licence to copy the contents. Reading published methods and citing points of comparison is different from turning another entrant's returned values or winning inputs into your submission. |
| May I publish the data? | The FAQ says to describe large datasets and link their external sources. Emeritus policy restricts reproducing programme or other students’ content unless expressly allowed. Check the dataset’s own terms and disclose any authorised external use in the README, datasheet and model card. |

I found no course-specific rule that explicitly approves or categorically
prohibits each form of pooling. The academic policy does give an applicable
general restriction. The question to get answered before relying on those data
for assessment is whether an entrant may use **uncoordinated, already-published
same-cohort or earlier-cohort input/output pairs** to fit surrogates or select
portal queries, and what attribution or disclosure the course requires. The
same question covers selecting an exact winning input from a public repo.

The Week 10 decision notebooks fit and score query candidates from the
official observations. They document Aby’s approach and pooled results as
comparisons. A decision whose rationale depends on an external score should be
described as externally informed, even if the final model trained on official
rows alone. The held-out F8 structural fit is reproducible from the official
starter observations. The quadratic hypothesis was investigated after the
published comparison.

The [Week 10 decision](../submissions/Week_10/README.md) records the revised
cohort-informed batch, the exact published F2 repeat and the official-data
fallback for assessed use.
